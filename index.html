Jetta transmission

Wreck Racing / Scan-based CAD reconstruction / Autodesk Fusion 360

Project objective

Create a CAD representation of transmission geometry from a 3D scan for integration into a twin-engine Volkswagen Jetta.

My contribution

I used the scan as a reference to reconstruct transmission components in Fusion 360. The comparison shows the scan beside the completed simplified model.

The model simplifies surface detail while retaining the overall housing form and selected openings and protrusions visible in the scan.

Autonomous robot

Component design / FDM manufacturing / Robot assembly

I designed the rear tower, upper coin guide, and underbody scoopers for a team robot operating within a $120 budget, size and energy limits, and a 40-second autonomous run.

Tower design: manufacturing constraints

The studio imposed a five-hour print limit. I divided the tower into two sections and added cutouts to reduce the material and print time required for each section.

I connected the sections with separate pins. Pins projecting from a tower section would have formed cantilevers in the print orientation, requiring supports and increasing print time.

Object collection geometry

Autonomous robot / Clearance, stiffness, and retention

The upper guide and underbody scoopers use fixed geometry to interact with objects as the robot moves. Their shapes had to account for the surrounding hardware and the robot's direction of travel.

Upper coin guide

Clearance and rigidity

The guide had to just clear a rotating cylinder while remaining rigid enough to force coins down the chute. These requirements constrained its position and shape: it needed to avoid interference while maintaining contact with the objects it directed.

The guide feeds a downstream chute that sorts objects by size.

Underbody scoopers

Pickup and retention on an incline

I varied the scoop angle to meet two operating requirements: collect an object during approach and retain it when the robot reverses up an incline.

As the robot travels onto the incline, the scoop tilts relative to gravity. I accounted for this change when selecting the scoop angle so the same geometry could support both pickup and retention.

Pile-driving instrumentation

Subsurface Processes Laboratory / Force measurement / Arduino

My research examines how driving frequency and mechanism angle affect pile-driving performance. I instrumented a stepper-motor-driven piston setup with a load cell and Arduino-based data acquisition to measure force.

Instrumentation and testing

I collect and compare force measurements at different driving frequencies to assess how motor speed affects pile penetration. The broader study also considers mechanism angle as a process variable.
